Features

All the safety plans for your project in one place

Start with the highlights, or pick a topic.

01 Templates

Don't start from a blank page

Choose from ten templates, from housing and high-rise to demolition, civil works and hydraulic engineering. Each template sets up the matching risk modules, with points of attention for that kind of work. Tick modules on or off before you start.

02 Risk matrix

See straight away which risk comes first

Rate each risk on likelihood and severity. The matrix runs from green to dark red and puts the risk in its place. Record measures for each risk, with a person responsible and a date.

03 Report

A plan you can print and share

With one click your health and safety plan becomes a tidy report: cover page, special risks and, per module, the risks with their measures. Print it or save it as a PDF for the project file.

04 BLVC plan

Keep the neighbourhood accessible and informed

When you work in public space, describe for each letter what you arrange: traffic diversions and emergency services, noise and dust, fencing and signage, and how residents get information and a point of contact.

05 Safe maintenance

A building that can be maintained safely later

Use a ten-point checklist to verify that the roof, facade, gutters, installations and lift can be reached safely. Keep a register of fixed provisions such as anchor points and guardrails, with inspection date and status.

Frequently asked questions

Which files can I open?

None yet. You start a plan from a template or fill it in yourself. Opening files and reading an IFC model will come in a later version.

Do I need to install anything?

No. Open Safety Studio runs in the browser, with no account. A desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux is in preparation but cannot be downloaded yet.

What does Open Safety Studio cost?

Nothing. The app is free, with no account or subscription.

In which format is my work saved?

Not yet: in this test version your input only stays while the window is open. Finish the plan and save the report as a PDF via Print. Saving as a project file will come in a later version.

Which systems does it run on?

In any modern browser on Windows, macOS and Linux. The app is made for a large screen.

Does it fit Dutch practice?

Yes. The health and safety plan follows article 2.28 of the Dutch Working Conditions Decree, the special risks come from Annex II of the European construction sites directive and the safe-maintenance checklist refers to the Dutch Building Decree and article 2.30 of the Working Conditions Decree. Measures follow the occupational hygiene hierarchy.

When is a health and safety plan required?

For work with a special risk from Annex II, or when the work lasts more than 30 working days with more than 20 people at once, or takes more than 500 person-days. The app shows this next to the plan.

Does it replace the health and safety coordinator?

No. The templates give a solid start, but the coordinator judges which risks and measures apply to this project and remains responsible for the plan.

Does it work with IFC models?

Not yet. There is an IFC tab, but importing, exporting and checking a model will come in a later version.

Technology

For those who want to know how it is built.

React 19 and TypeScript

The whole interface, including the forms, the matrix and the report.

Vite

Fast build for the web version at open-safety-studio.open-aec.com.

Tauri 2

A thin native Rust shell for the desktop app in preparation.

Templates as data

Templates and risk modules are typed data in the code, so they are easy to extend.

HTML report

The report is a self-contained HTML document that the browser prints or saves as PDF.

OpenAEC shell

Ribbon, themes and translations (i18next) as in all OpenAEC apps.
